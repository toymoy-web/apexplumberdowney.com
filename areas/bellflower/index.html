What Is in the Water

One published report, and what it shows

Liberty Utilities publishes a combined report for its Bellflower and Norwalk system. Its 2023 data, released in 2024, says about 87 percent of that supply was imported Metropolitan Water District water and about 13 percent came from Central Basin wells. The imported water averaged about 9.4 grains per gallon of hardness, with a range of 5.8 to 12.9, and the local wells averaged about 22.8.

That report speaks only for Liberty's customers. The other three companies publish their own reports, and we have not confirmed their numbers, so we do not print one for the whole city. If scale is a regular fight in your home, our water softener page explains how to size a unit once you know your own hardness.

The imported water carries chloramine, which Liberty's report says is not a health hazard to the general population but needs attention from dialysis patients and fish owners. If your tank heater is failing, scale is the usual suspect, and water heater repair starts with a flush and an anode check.

Before We Arrive

If you have a burst pipe or active leak

Water that is loose in a Bellflower home does its worst damage in the first ten minutes. Four quick moves slow it down and leave you with photos a landlord or insurer will accept.

1

Find the main shutoff

Homes in this city often keep the main valve by the front hose bib, at the side of the house, or in the garage. Close a round handle clockwise or swing a lever across the pipe, and do not force a stuck one.

2

Cut the power nearby

A wet ceiling light, a soaked outlet, or a flooded water heater closet means the breaker for that area should go off. Reach the panel only if the floor around it is dry.

3

Protect what you can

Pick up rugs, boxes, and electronics, then take dated photos of the source and the damage. If you live in a duplex or fourplex, warn the unit below because water finds the lowest room.

4

Call the dispatch line

Dial (562) 488-9614 and give us your street plus the nearest cross street. Say whether the water is warm or cool, since warm usually points at the heater and cool at a supply line.
