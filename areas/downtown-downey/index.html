Plumber in Downtown Downey, CA

Plumbing for homes, shops and apartments around Downey Avenue and Third Street, where the City plan puts its downtown.

  • Downey Avenue and Third St

Downtown Downey is where the city keeps its City Hall, its library and its theaters, and it is also a place where people live. The City of Downey adopted a Downtown Downey Specific Plan on September 28, 2010. The plan covers about 131 acres and divides them into five districts, centered on the corner of Downey Avenue and Third Street.

That mix is what shapes the plumbing here. A single block can hold a restaurant, an office, a small apartment building and a house, and each has different pipes and a different person responsible for them. Below is a plain guide to the plan's districts and what each one means when water goes wrong.

Why It Matters

Apartments over shops and shared lines

The plan allows apartments and condos above ground floor businesses in the Downtown Core, and it calls for the same arrangement along the Firestone Boulevard corridor. Wherever a building stacks homes over a shop, pipes run through walls and ceilings that more than one household or business depends on. That is general plumbing, not a claim about any particular building here.

It also means the first question on a downtown call is simple. Whose pipe is it? A leak in a ceiling can start in the unit above.

A slow drain on the ground floor can come from a shared line. Before any work, we ask for the street, the building type and whether you are the owner, a tenant or a property manager, because that decides who approves the repair.

Who To Call

City side and building side

The City's Utilities Services Division handles the water system and the sewer mains and lists (562) 904-7202, with office hours Monday to Thursday 6:30 a.m. to 4:00 p.m. and Friday 6:30 a.m. to 3:00 p.m. If your whole block has no water or several neighbors see the same sewer backup, that call comes first. The page we checked lists no after-hours number, so ask when you call.

If only your building is affected, the problem is on the building's side, and that is where a plumber comes in. For a water heater swap, the City's Building and Safety FAQ lists replaced water heaters among the work that generally needs a permit. The Building Division is at 562-904-7142.
