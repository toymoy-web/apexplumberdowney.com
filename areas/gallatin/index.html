History

Where Gallatin came from

The Downey Patriot describes Gallatin as a small village near today's Paramount Boulevard and Florence Avenue. Settlers built a small schoolhouse of tule reeds and brush in the late 1860s, and a two-story schoolhouse replaced it in 1871. Gallatin, College Settlement and Downey merged by 1883, when the Southern Pacific Railroad agreed to stop in Downey.

The school moved in 1893 to the corner of Brookshire Avenue and Gallatin Road. A 1933 earthquake severely damaged the building, and the current Spanish colonial style building opened in 1936. Early accounts describe dairy farms and orange groves around the school, which is a reminder that this was farmland before it was a neighborhood.

What We Can Say About Plumbing

General guidance for a Gallatin home

We will not guess what is inside your walls based on a neighborhood name. A house's age, its pipe material and its past repairs are specific to the address. The useful step is to find out your own facts. The Los Angeles County Assessor's property page lists the year built for your address, and a plumber can camera the sewer line and check the supply pipes and pressure before you decide on a repair or a purchase.

For leaks, the meter tells you quickly. Close every fixture, then watch the red flow triangle on the meter. The City of Downey's meter-leak page says a detected leak should be repaired within 48 hours. If the triangle moves with your main closed, the leak is in the line between the meter and the house.

Who To Call

The City and the plumber

For a street-wide water outage or a sewer main backup, start with the City's Utilities Services Division at (562) 904-7202, with office hours Monday to Thursday 6:30 a.m. to 4:00 p.m. and Friday 6:30 a.m. to 3:00 p.m. The page we checked lists no after-hours number. For anything inside your property line, such as a leaking supply pipe, a failed water heater, or a blocked house line, that is what a plumber is for.
