Water Through Whittier

How Santa Fe Springs gets its supply

The Santa Fe Springs Water Utility Authority's 2024 report says it obtains treated groundwater through the City of Whittier from deep wells in the Whittier Narrows area. It also receives treated groundwater from the Central Basin Water Quality Protection Program facility, again through Whittier, and filtered surface water from the Metropolitan Water District.

For a building owner, the useful point is that supply and pressure notices come from the Santa Fe Springs utility, even though some of the water travels through Whittier's system. The 2024 report does not publish a hardness figure, so we do not quote one. If scale is a problem at your site, a test strip at the tap tells you what you are dealing with, and our salt-based softener page explains sizing.

Before We Arrive

If you have a burst pipe or active leak

A burst pipe or active leak at a Santa Fe Springs business costs money every hour. These four steps help you contain it, whether you run a shop, a kitchen, or a warehouse.

1

Shut off the main valve

Know the building's main shutoff and any separate valve for the restroom block or kitchen. A tenant should ask the property manager where each one is before there is an emergency.

2

Kill power to wet areas

If water reaches equipment, a panel, or a lighting circuit, switch off that circuit only if you can do so from dry ground. Never stand in water to reach a panel.

3

Clear the area

Keep staff and customers away from standing water and move stock off wet floors. Photograph the damage and tell the landlord and your insurer.
