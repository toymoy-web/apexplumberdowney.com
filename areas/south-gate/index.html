South Gate's Wells

Where the water comes from, and why it stains

The City of South Gate runs its own water system with about 14,400 service connections, supplied by local deep groundwater wells. Its 2021 report lists total hardness at an average of 226.3 milligrams per liter, with a range of 200 to 250. That works out to about 13 grains per gallon, which is hard.

The same report says one well exceeded the secondary limit for manganese and is treated for iron and manganese. The report states that manganese limits exist to protect against stains on laundry and plumbing fixtures. If you see brown or orange marks in tubs and toilets, the water and the pipes are both worth checking, and a toilet that stains at the waterline is a common first clue.

Before We Arrive

If you have a burst pipe or active leak

When water is loose in a South Gate house, the first two minutes matter more than the next two hours. Older homes make the first step harder, so here is how to handle it.

1

Shut off the main valve

Many older South Gate homes have a gate valve with a round wheel that has not turned in years. Turn it clockwise slowly, and if it will not move, close the shutoff under the sink or behind the toilet and keep calling.

2

Kill power to wet areas

If water is near a light fixture, an outlet, or a garage panel, switch off power to that area only when you can reach the breaker dry. Never stand in water to reach a panel.

3

Clear the area

In a duplex or an apartment, knock on the neighbor's door below and tell the property manager, because water travels. Lift furniture off wet floors and photograph the damage.

4

Call our 24/7 line
