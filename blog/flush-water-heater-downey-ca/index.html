How to Flush a Water Heater in Downey, CA (Step by Step)

A step-by-step guide for homeowners, plus what the local water reports say about why it matters.

By the Downey Apex Plumbing team · Updated October 12, 2026

To flush a tank water heater, turn off the power or gas, close the cold water valve, attach a hose to the drain valve at the bottom, open a hot tap in the house, and drain the tank until the water runs clear. Then close the drain, refill the tank, and restore power. It takes about an hour, and in hard-water areas like ours it is one of the most useful bits of maintenance a homeowner can do.

The reason is sediment. Minerals in hard water settle on the bottom of the tank, insulate the water from the burner or lower element, and use up the anode rod that protects the steel. Flushing sweeps some of that out before it hardens into a layer you cannot remove.

Before You Start

What you need and what to check

A short checklist

  • 1A garden hose long enough to reach a floor drain or the yard.
  • 2Work gloves. The water can be very hot.
  • 3A flathead screwdriver or a bucket, in case the drain valve is slow.
  • 4The tank's age on the data plate. Past about ten years, consider calling a plumber.
  • 5Time. Do not rush a hot tank.
Step by Step

How to flush the tank

1

Power off

Switch off the breaker for electric, or set gas to pilot or off.

2

Water off

Close the cold supply valve on top of the tank.

3

Connect and open

Run a hose from the drain valve and open a hot tap.

4

Drain and rinse

Drain the tank, then briefly open the cold supply to stir the sediment.

Keep draining until the water from the hose runs clear. Close the drain valve, remove the hose, and close the hot tap you opened. Open the cold supply and let the tank fill.

Leave a hot tap open until water flows steadily, which shows the tank is full. Only then restore power or relight gas according to the label. Turning on an electric element in an empty tank burns it out.

Hard Water

Why flushing matters here

Golden State Water's 2026 report for the Norwalk System lists 2025 hardness of 8.0 to 30 grains per gallon, with an average of 18. The City of South Gate's 2021 report puts its average near 13. These are hard-water numbers, and a tank in water like that collects sediment faster than one in a soft-water city.

For Downey, the city's current water-quality report is the place to confirm the exact figure. Whatever the number, the routine is the same. Flush once a year, check the anode rod every few years, and replace a tank that is past its prime.

Know the Limits

When to leave it to a plumber

Call a plumber if the tank is leaking, if the drain valve is plastic and old, if the heater is in an attic or tight closet, or if you smell gas. A flush is a maintenance task, not a repair. If the tank is rusting at the base or dripping from the body, flushing will not fix it.

Also check that the heater is strapped to the wall. California requires bracing against earthquakes, and a plumber can add straps while on site.

Checking the Anode Rod

A small part with a big job

The anode rod is a sacrificial metal rod that corrodes so the steel tank does not. In hard water it wears faster. To check it, shut off the power and water, drain a few gallons, and use a socket wrench to unscrew the hex head on top of the heater.

A rod that is mostly bare steel wire needs replacing. Replacing it can extend the life of a tank by years.

Some tanks have limited clearance above them, and the rod needs room to pull out. In that case a plumber can fit a flexible rod. Plan the check every three years, or sooner in very hard water.

Signs your tank is past flushing

  • 1Rusty water from the hot tap only.
  • 2Moisture or rust around the base.
  • 3A tank over ten years old with loud rumbling.
  • 4Repeated pilot or element failures.
  • 5Hot water that runs out far faster than it used to.

When the signs add up, a flush buys a little time and a replacement buys certainty. A tank that fails on its own schedule usually fails at the worst moment, and the water that comes out of it stays in your house.
