What Not to Put Down a Garbage Disposal in Downey, CA

A sorted list of what jams, clogs, or coats your pipes, with what to do instead.

By the Downey Apex Plumbing team · Updated October 12, 2026

Do not put grease, oil, fibrous vegetables like celery and corn husks, bones in quantity, coffee grounds in bulk, pasta and rice, or non-food items down a garbage disposal. A disposal grinds soft scraps and sends them down the drain. It does not dissolve anything, and the things that do the most damage are the ones that cool, swell, or tangle after they leave the grinder.

A little care with the sink saves a clogged kitchen line, a burned-out motor, and a service call. The table below sorts the common items.

Why Grease Is the Worst

What happens after it leaves the sink

Grease looks harmless as a liquid. It cools in the pipe, sticks to the wall, and catches food scraps. Over weeks the line narrows. On a larger scale, fats, oils, and grease are a recognized cause of sewer blockages, and many sewer agencies ask households and businesses to keep them out of drains.

Hard water adds to it. Mineral scale roughens the pipe, and grease and scale together build a coating a cable cannot remove. That is when high-pressure jetting comes into the picture. Golden State Water's Norwalk average hardness was about 18 grains per gallon in 2025, and the City of South Gate's was about 13 in 2021, so scale is part of the picture in this area.

Good Habits

A short routine for a happy disposal

Five habits that help

  • 1Run cold water before, during, and for about fifteen seconds after grinding.
  • 2Feed scraps in small amounts, not a whole plate at once.
  • 3Scrape plates into the trash first, and use the disposal for soft leftovers.
  • 4Grind a few ice cubes and a citrus peel now and then to clean the grinder.
  • 5Never put your hand in the disposal, and switch it off before checking a jam.
When It Breaks

Jams, leaks, and bad smells

A jam usually clears with the hex key socket on the bottom of the unit and the reset button. A leak from the bottom means a cracked housing, and the unit needs replacing. Our garbage disposal repair page explains how we decide. If the sink backs up even when the disposal is off, the problem is in the drain line, and our drain unclogging service takes it from there.

In Rentals and Older Homes

Where it comes up most

In rentals, where several tenants share a drain stack, grease from one unit can block the line for all. About 57 percent of Paramount's occupied homes are rented by the 2020 Census, and about 55 percent in South Gate. If you manage a building, a note to tenants about what not to put down the sink is cheap insurance. We handle kitchen drain calls in Paramount, CA and the neighboring cities.

Cleaning It

A five-minute refresh

1

Switch off

Turn the disposal off at the switch and unplug if you can.

2

Scrub the splash guard

Use a brush and dish soap on the rubber flaps.

3

Grind ice

A cup of ice cubes knocks residue off the grinders.

4

Rinse with cold

Run cold water for twenty seconds, then a citrus peel.

Never put a hand into a disposal, even with the switch off. Use tongs or pliers to remove an object, and use the hex key from below to free a jam. Keep fingers well clear.

A disposal is a small appliance with a short list of jobs. Keeping the rules simple, soft scraps in and everything else out, extends its life and keeps your drain line clear.

A note for landlords and managers

  • 1Post a short list of what not to put down the sink.
  • 2Offer a reminder at move-in.
  • 3Check disposals at turnover.
  • 4Keep a record of repairs.
  • 5Act promptly on slow drains in shared stacks.

A small container by the sink for scraps makes the habit easy. Wipe greasy pans with a paper towel before washing, and pour cooled fat into a jar or can for the trash. These are small changes, and together they stop most disposal and kitchen drain problems.

One more habit worth building is a weekly rinse. Run hot water for a minute after washing greasy dishes, and wipe the sink walls so residue does not dry on them. A last tip: if you have a septic system, the rules are different, and many households in the wider region are on sewer, but check which you have.

Septic tanks are sensitive to grease and chemicals, and a disposal adds solids to the tank. In our service area homes are connected to public sewers, which still benefit from the same careful habits.

Disposals are listed to safety standards such as UL 430 and wired under the National Electrical Code (NEC), so a replacement should be installed with proper wiring and a switch you can reach. If yours is hardwired and you are replacing it, have a qualified person handle the connection.
