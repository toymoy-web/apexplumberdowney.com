Hard Water in Downey and the Cities Around It: What the Reports Say

Official hardness figures from Norwalk, South Gate, and Paramount, lined up on one scale, with the gaps stated honestly.

By the Downey Apex Plumbing team · Updated October 3, 2026

Water in the cities around Downey ranges from hard to very hard, and the official numbers differ by utility. Golden State Water reported an average of about 18 grains per gallon in Norwalk for 2025, with a range of 8 to 30. The City of South Gate's 2021 report averages about 13.

Paramount's 2018 report puts its well water near 11. This article lines those figures up, explains the scale used to rank them, and says what they mean for your water heater.

Every number below comes from a utility's own report. Where we could not find an official figure, we say so instead of guessing.

What It Means

Scale in your water heater and fixtures

Hard water leaves scale. In a tank water heater, scale settles as sediment, insulates the water from the burner, and uses up the anode rod. Our guide to flushing a tank explains the routine, and our hot water heater repair page covers when a tank is past saving. On faucets and showerheads, scale shows as white crust and weak flow.

The cure for scale is a softener, which removes the minerals. The cure for the symptoms is regular maintenance. A softener is worth considering when the average sits above about 10 grains per gallon. Our softener installation and repair page includes a sizing calculator that uses the figures above.

A Note on Downey

The gap we are being open about

Downey's water comes from 20 deep groundwater wells in the Central Basin, with three imported connections from the Metropolitan Water District as backup. Both sources carry minerals, so Downey's water is hard, and the neighboring official figures above suggest the range. The City of Downey Water Division's annual report is the authority. If you want to size a softener or tune a water heater routine, use that report or a test strip at your tap. We also cover the water in Norwalk in more detail, since its report is the most complete.

Other ways hardness shows up

  • 1White crust on faucets and showerheads.
  • 2Spots on glassware after the dishwasher.
  • 3Soap that does not lather well.
  • 4Stiff laundry and dull towels.
  • 5A water heater that runs out of hot water sooner each year.

Salt-free conditioners do not remove hardness minerals. They change how scale forms, and results vary. If your goal is to stop scale, a salt-based softener removes the minerals, but check local rules about brine discharge before you buy.

Reading Your Own Report

Where to look in a utility's annual report

1

Find the table

Look for a section called Other Parameters or Additional Constituents of Interest.

2

Spot hardness

It is listed as total hardness, in mg/L or grains per gallon.

3

Check the range

Note the minimum and maximum, not just the average.

4

Check the year

Use the most recent report, and note the sampling date.

If the report gives milligrams per liter, divide by 17.1 for grains per gallon. A figure of 171 mg/L is 10 grains per gallon, for example. Many reports list hardness in a table near the end, and the numbers are easy to miss if you only read the first pages.

If you are shopping for a softener or a water heater, bring the figure from your own utility report to the conversation. A good installer will ask for it, and it changes the recommendation. A household at 18 grains per gallon needs a larger unit or more frequent regeneration than one at 11, and the maintenance schedule for the water heater tightens as the number rises.

Practical steps cost little. Request your utility's report, test your tap with a strip, and call a technician if your water heater is more than eight years old. If scale is already damaging fixtures, ask for a repair estimate for the worst of them and a price for a softener, then compare. According to the utility reports cited above, the smart move in every one of these cities is to plan for scale instead of reacting to it.
