A Renter's Guide to Plumbing Repairs in California

Who fixes what, how to report a problem, and where the common pitfalls are, written for tenants in Downey and nearby cities.

By the Downey Apex Plumbing team · Updated October 3, 2026

If you rent in California and your plumbing breaks, your landlord is generally responsible for keeping it in working order under the state's habitability rules. The practical steps are to report the problem in writing, take photos, give the landlord a reasonable time to respond, and call a plumber yourself if there is an emergency and the landlord cannot be reached. This is general information, not legal advice.

Plumbing problems in rentals often drag on because nobody is sure whose job it is. The notes below help you keep a clear record, which is the most useful thing a tenant can have.

A simple routine for reporting a problem

  • 1Take photos and a short video of the problem, with the date visible if you can.
  • 2Write to the landlord by text or email. Say what is wrong, where, and since when.
  • 3Keep a copy and note when you sent it.
  • 4Follow up if there is no response within a reasonable time.
  • 5If water is spreading, shut it off and call a plumber, then tell the landlord what you did.

Common Problems

What tenants call about most

A slow drain is the most common request. Try a plunger or a drain screen first, and ask the landlord to arrange blocked drain cleaning if it keeps happening. A leaking faucet or toilet is next. A dripping tap wastes water every day, and a landlord usually prefers a quick fix to a larger bill.

A water heater that fails is a bigger problem, because no hot water is a habitability issue. Report it right away. If you smell gas, leave and call the gas utility first.

For Landlords

Why quick repairs save money

If you own a rental, the cheapest plumbing job is the one done the day it is reported. A faucet washer costs little, and a faucet left to leak for a season can rot a cabinet. Schedule repairs promptly and keep records of what you fixed and when. Our faucet cartridge replacement page explains the usual fixes, and we work with property managers across the area.

None of this replaces advice from an attorney or a tenant organization. If your situation is a dispute, get that advice first.

A Sample Notice

What a good written report includes

Elements of a plumbing repair request

  • 1Your name, address, and unit number.
  • 2The date you are writing.
  • 3A plain description of the problem and where it is.
  • 4The date you first noticed it.
  • 5A request to repair it, and your contact information.
  • 6Photos attached, and a note that you are keeping a copy.

Keep the tone factual and calm. A short message such as the following works: "The kitchen faucet has been leaking since September 28. Water collects under the sink.

Photos attached. Please let me know when someone can repair it." It does not accuse anyone, and it creates a dated record.

If you hear nothing, follow up in writing after a reasonable interval. A series of dated messages is far more helpful than an oral conversation if a dispute arises later.

Resources exist for tenants who need more than general information. Local tenant organizations and legal aid groups can explain your rights and your options. We are plumbers, so we stick to the plumbing, but we are glad to write down exactly what we found when a landlord or a tenant asks.

A final point for tenants: keep your own copy of the lease and any move-in inspection report. If a plumbing problem existed when you moved in, the move-in report is your proof. If you caused damage by accident, say so honestly and early, because an honest report is far easier to resolve than a hidden one. Landlords and tenants both want a working house, and a calm paper trail helps more than an argument.

According to the habitability rules summarized above, working plumbing is part of what a rental must provide. The California Plumbing Code, built on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), is the standard a plumber follows when repairing it, so a plumbing repair done properly should meet that code. Ask the landlord to confirm who is doing the work and whether a permit applies.
