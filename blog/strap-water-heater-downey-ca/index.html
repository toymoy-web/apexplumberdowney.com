How to Strap a Water Heater in Downey, CA

The parts, the steps, and the setups that need a professional, for homes in earthquake country.

By the Downey Apex Plumbing team · Updated October 31, 2026

To strap a water heater in California, you anchor the tank to wall studs with two metal straps, one in the upper third of the tank and one in the lower third, each secured with lag screws into the framing. The straps stop the tank from tipping during an earthquake, which protects the gas and water lines and prevents flooding or fire. It is a one-hour job with a kit that costs little, and it is required.

If you are buying a house or replacing a heater, check that the straps are in place. A tipped tank can break its connections, and a gas leak plus a pilot flame is the combination you do not want after a quake.

What You Need

Parts and tools

Gather these before you start

  • 1A water heater strapping kit sized for your tank.
  • 2A stud finder and a pencil.
  • 3A drill and a socket set for the lag screws.
  • 4A level, to mark the strap height.
  • 5Gloves and safety glasses.
Step by Step

How a standard strapping job goes

1

Find the studs

Mark two studs behind the tank.

2

Mark the heights

Upper strap at the top third, lower strap at the bottom third, above the controls.

3

Wrap and anchor

Run the straps around the tank and lag-screw them into the studs.

4

Tighten and check

The tank should not move when you push it.

In Southern California

Why bracing is not optional here

The California Plumbing Code, built on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), expects a water heater to be braced against seismic movement. Southern California sits on active faults, and a tank that tips can tear its own lines. A gas-fired tank that falls is a fire risk, and a full tank that falls is a flood risk.

In homes built in the 1950s, which make up roughly 13,400 Downey homes by census-based counts, the heater may have been replaced several times without anyone checking the straps. A quick look takes a minute. When we replace a heater, bracing is part of the job. Our new water heater installation page explains the rest of a proper install.

After an Earthquake

A short checklist for the plumbing

What to check once the shaking stops

  • 1If you smell gas or hear hissing, leave the house. Then call 911 and your gas utility from outside.
  • 2Look at the water heater for leaning, leaks, or loosened connections.
  • 3If water is leaking inside the house, close the main water valve.
  • 4Do not relight a pilot after a quake until the gas is confirmed safe.
  • 5Photograph any damage for your insurer.

If you smell gas and cannot reach a plumber, leave first and call the gas utility. Our gas line repair page explains what we do afterward. We also help homeowners in Paramount with bracing and connector upgrades.

Buy a kit, not loose parts, unless you know exactly what you need. A kit pairs the straps with the right hardware. If your tank is in a closet or on a raised platform, ask a plumber to review the setup, because the platform itself must be secure.

When you replace a heater, bracing should be part of the job. Ask the installer to show you the straps after they finish, and take a photo for your records.

A checklist when buying a home

  • 1Look at the water heater and confirm two straps are in place.
  • 2Push the tank gently. It should not move.
  • 3Check the date on the data plate. Older than ten years suggests replacement soon.
  • 4Look for flexible connectors on the gas and water lines.
  • 5Ask the seller for any permit paperwork on the heater.

Bracing does not make a tank earthquake-proof, but it makes a tipped tank, a torn line, and a flood or fire far less likely. For a one-hour job, that is a good trade.

A Scenario

A heater in a hall closet

Picture a 1950s house where the water heater sits in a hall closet with the door removed to make room. The tank is against a drywall wall, with studs 16 inches apart behind it. The straps go into those studs, not into the drywall alone, and the lag screws need a pilot hole so they do not split the wood.

If the closet has a raised wooden platform, as some do, the platform needs to be fastened to the floor and the wall. A strap that holds a tank to a loose platform is only as strong as the platform. A plumber can check this in a few minutes and fix it while there.

The same house probably has a rigid copper or galvanized line to the heater. Swapping it for a flexible connector is inexpensive and gives the tank room to move without tearing the pipe, and it is a good job to do the same afternoon as the straps.

Downey Note

Strapping rules and permits in Downey

California Health and Safety Code section 19211 requires new and replacement water heaters to be braced, anchored, or strapped against earthquake motion. That is state law, so it applies in Downey as it does everywhere in California.

The City of Downey's Building and Safety FAQ lists replaced water heaters among the work that generally needs a permit. Call the Building Division at 562-904-7142 to confirm for your job. Neighbors in South Gate, CA and Norwalk, CA should ask their own city.
