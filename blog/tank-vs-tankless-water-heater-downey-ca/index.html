Tank vs Tankless Water Heaters in Downey, CA

A plain comparison, the questions that decide it, and how hard water and gas lines change the answer.

By the Downey Apex Plumbing team · Updated October 12, 2026

For most Southern California homes, a standard tank water heater is the lower-cost choice up front, and a tankless unit is the better choice if you want endless hot water, have the gas line and venting for it, and will descale it every year. A heat pump tank is the efficient electric option for homes with space and open air. The right pick depends on your household, your fuel, and your closet, not on a single winner.

Below are the questions that decide it, in the order a plumber would ask them. Answer them honestly and the choice usually makes itself.

Hard Water

Why it matters for the choice

Hard water leaves scale in the heat exchanger of a tankless unit, which cuts flow and triggers error codes. Our tankless descaling page explains the yearly flush. A tank collects sediment too, and a yearly flush helps it as well.

Official figures nearby make the point. Golden State Water reported an average of about 18 grains per gallon in Norwalk for 2025, and the City of South Gate's 2021 report averages about 13. Check the City of Downey's current water-quality report for your own figure, and whichever unit you choose, plan for maintenance. A softener reduces how often descaling is needed.

The Gas Side

What a tankless upgrade can require

Tankless units use more gas at once than a tank does, so the gas line and the meter must keep up. Older homes, which include roughly 13,400 Downey houses from the 1950s by census-based counts, often have gas lines sized for the appliances of that era. A load check tells us whether the line is enough. Our gas line installation page covers upgrades.

Venting is the other half. A tankless unit needs the right vent material and routing, and an inspection under the California Plumbing Code, built on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC). We explain what applies before we quote.

A Fair Way to Decide

Our rule of thumb

Lean toward a tank when

  • The budget is tight
  • Hot water use is modest
  • The gas line is old and small
  • Floor space is not an issue

Lean toward tankless when

  • You want endless hot water
  • You can accept yearly descaling
  • The gas line and vent can handle it
  • Space is limited

The surprise for many tankless owners is the yearly flush. It takes about an hour and some descaling solution, and skipping it in hard water is the main way to shorten the life of the unit. A tank needs a flush too, but a missed tank flush is more forgiving than a missed tankless flush.

When you compare quotes, ask about maintenance as well as price. A unit that costs more and needs more care may not be the better value for a household that will not do the upkeep.

There is no wrong answer if the choice matches your household. A good installer will ask about hot water use, gas supply, space, and budget before recommending anything, and will tell you if the unit you prefer is a poor fit.

Two Households

How the choice plays out

Take a family of five in a 1950s house with a standard tank in the garage. They run laundry, dishes, and showers back to back in the morning, and the tank runs out. A larger tank or a second unit may fix it for less than a tankless conversion that needs a new gas line. A tankless unit makes sense if they also want to free the floor space.

Now take a couple in a small house with a tank in a tight closet. They use little hot water, so a tankless unit would save modestly on gas, and a wall-mounted unit would free the closet. The deciding factor is whether the gas line and vent route work, which a plumber can confirm in one visit.

In both cases the answer came from the house, not from a general rule. That is the point of the questions earlier in this article, and it is why we ask them before we quote.
