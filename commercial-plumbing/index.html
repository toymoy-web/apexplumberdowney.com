Commercial plumbing in Downey, CA covers the pipes, drains, fixtures, and water heaters that keep a business open: restaurants, shops, offices, and small multi-tenant buildings. Call the 24/7 line when a drain backs up or a pipe breaks, and a plumber will tell you what the problem is and what it costs before repairs begin.

Commercial calls differ from home calls in three ways. The stakes are higher because a closed business loses money by the hour. The systems are larger, with grease lines, floor drains, and bigger water heaters. And more than one party is usually involved, from the owner to the tenant to the utility.

Grease and Floor Drains

Why food businesses have their own plumbing problems

A kitchen sends fats, oils, and food solids down its drains all day. They cool and coat the pipe, and over weeks the line narrows. A cable can open a channel, but the coating remains, so the clog comes back. That is the situation our hydrojetting page describes.

Many local sewer agencies require grease traps or interceptors for food service and set service schedules. We cannot tell you the rule for your site from here. We can inspect the unit you have, tell you what condition it is in, and point you to the agency that sets the schedule.

Who Pays

Landlords, tenants, and the pipe

Leases usually split responsibility. Fixtures inside a suite often belong to the tenant, and building systems such as risers and the main line often belong to the owner. When a repair costs money, the question is where in the system the problem sits.

We can tell you that. We trace the problem, explain which part of the plumbing is involved, and write that down so you and the other party start from the same facts. We do not read leases, and for a dispute you should speak with the property owner or an attorney.

For a business owner, the useful point is that older commercial buildings and newer ones differ. A strip center built decades ago may share a drain line among tenants, while newer buildings may have separate lines. We ask early which you have, because it decides who needs to know when a line backs up. Commercial properties often also need annual backflow testing, which our backflow testing and prevention page explains.

Code

Rules that apply to commercial work

Commercial plumbing follows the California Plumbing Code, which is built on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), along with local sewer rules and the accessibility requirements in the California Building Code. Restroom fixture counts and accessibility features depend on the type of occupancy. Work normally needs a permit and an inspection. We tell you what applies before we quote.
