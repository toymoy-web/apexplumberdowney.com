Downey Water

Why scale shows up on Downey faucets

The City of Downey Water Division's supply is hard. Minerals dry out as the water evaporates, leaving the white crust on spouts and showerheads. They also collect inside aerators and cartridges, which is why flow gets weak over time.

The result is that faucets here often fail from scale before they wear out. Cleaning an aerator every few months and replacing cartridges before they seize is an inexpensive habit. If scale is a theme in your house, a water softener installation addresses it for every fixture.

The California Plumbing Code, built on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), expects an accessible shutoff valve at each fixture. Houses from the 1950s, which make up roughly 13,400 Downey homes, often have the original valves still under the sink. Those valves tend to seize and leak when turned, so the repair visit is a good time to replace them.

A simple maintenance routine

  • 1Unscrew and soak the aerator in white vinegar every few months, then rinse.
  • 2Open the cabinet under each sink twice a year and look for moisture or stains.
  • 3Turn each shutoff valve a quarter turn and back, so it does not seize.
  • 4Replace braided supply lines about every ten years, or sooner if they show corrosion.
  • 5Fix a drip promptly. It rarely stays small.
