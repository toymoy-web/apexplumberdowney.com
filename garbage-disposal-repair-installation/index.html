What to do if your disposal stops

  • 1Turn off the switch. Unplug the disposal if there is a plug under the sink.
  • 2Do not put your hand in the disposal, even with the power off.
  • 3Look at the bottom for a hex-key socket. Work a hex key back and forth to free a jam.
  • 4Press the red reset button on the bottom of the unit.
  • 5If it hums and will not spin, or trips the breaker, stop and call.

Garbage disposal repair and installation in Downey, CA covers jams, leaks, noise, clogs, and new installs. Many problems are simple, but a leaking or cracked unit usually means replacement. Call us when the disposal will not restart, when water leaks from the body, or when the sink backs up.

A kitchen sink that will not drain is often blamed on the disposal when the real problem is the drain line beyond it. We check both before recommending a fix.

Repair when

  • The unit is only a few years old
  • The problem is a jam or a loose connection
  • There is no leak from the body
  • The motor runs smoothly

Replace when

  • Water leaks from the housing
  • The motor burns out or trips the breaker repeatedly
  • The unit is near ten years old
  • You want more power or quieter operation

Why disposals struggle in older homes

Roughly 13,400 Downey homes were built in the 1950s, and many have had kitchens remodeled since. The sink drain in those houses was not designed for a disposal's heavier output, and a partly narrowed branch line backs up faster. If your disposal works but the sink drains slowly, the problem may be in the line.

Hard water adds a second layer. Where water is hard, mineral scale can coat the grinding surfaces and drain walls. Grease and soap bind with that scale and form a coating that catches food scraps.

The National Electrical Code (NEC) governs the wiring that powers a disposal, and the California Plumbing Code governs the trap and drain connection. We install to both and test the unit before we leave.

Garbage disposal questions from Downey

It is jammed. Turn the switch off and, if there is a wall plug under the sink, unplug it. Insert a hex key into the socket in the center of the bottom and work it back and forth to free the flywheel, then press the red reset button on the bottom. Never put a hand in the disposal.

Check where. Water from the top is usually the sink flange seal. Water from the side points to the drain or dishwasher hose connection. Water from the bottom usually means the housing has cracked or an internal seal has failed, and that means replacement.

A new disposal has a plug in the dishwasher inlet. If the plug was not knocked out during installation, the dishwasher cannot drain through the disposal. It is a common installation error and a quick fix.

Eight to twelve years is common, depending on the model and use. Grease, fibrous scraps, and overloading shorten that. Hard water can leave scale on the grinding components over time.

A correct installation includes a proper trap, secure mounting, safe wiring, and a dishwasher connection with a high loop or air gap. Disposals carry safety certification such as UL 430, and the wiring follows the National Electrical Code (NEC).
