Hose Bib & Outdoor Faucet Repair in Downey, CA

Fixes for dripping, stuck, and leaking outdoor faucets, with a vacuum breaker so your hose cannot pull water back into the house.

Hose bib and outdoor faucet repair in Downey, CA covers dripping spigots, leaks at the handle, stuck valves, missing vacuum breakers, and water that appears where the pipe meets the wall. Most repairs are small, but a hose bib that leaks inside the wall can quietly damage a house, so it is worth acting on early.

Outdoor faucets live a hard life. They sit in the sun, collect scale, and often get a hose left attached for months. Here in Southern California freezing is rarely the problem. Wear is.

What to do if water is leaking behind the faucet

  • 1Close the main shutoff, or the shutoff valve for the outdoor line if you have one.
  • 2Look at the wall inside the house, in the garage or crawlspace, for dampness.
  • 3Photograph any staining or soft drywall.
  • 4Do not turn the faucet on again until it has been checked.
  • 5Call us. A leak inside a wall is cheaper to fix early.

Backflow and Irrigation

The small cap that protects your drinking water

A hose left in a bucket, a pool, or a puddle of fertilizer can draw that water back into your pipes if pressure drops, a problem called backflow. A hose bib vacuum breaker is a small, inexpensive fitting that prevents it. Golden State Water's 2026 report for its Norwalk System advises installing one on every faucet where a hose can connect.

Sprinkler systems carry a bigger risk and usually need a testable backflow assembly. The rules come from your water utility, and our backflow testing page explains the process.

Pressure, scale, and older faucets

Where water is hard, scale builds up inside outdoor faucets and makes handles stiff over time. The City of Downey Water Division targets about 65 psi at its water yard, and pressure at a house can run higher. The California Plumbing Code, built on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), expects a regulator above 80 psi, because high pressure strains every fitting, including the one outside.

Census-based counts put roughly 13,400 Downey homes in the 1950s, and many still have their original outdoor faucets. If yours is that old, replacing it during a repair is usually the better value. A wet patch on the lawn that is not near the faucet may be a line leak, which our leak finding service finds.
