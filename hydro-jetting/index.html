Start With the Problem

When a drain keeps clogging after it has been snaked

A drain that clears for a few weeks and then slows again is telling you the clog was never the whole story. Water drains through the opening a cable made, but the grease, soap, and mineral film on the pipe wall is still there, and it keeps catching new debris.

Another version of the same problem is a cable that cannot get through, or stops partway and will not advance. That can mean a heavy build-up the machine cannot cut, or a bend or break the cable cannot pass. In both cases, guessing at a second pass wastes time. A camera run comes before the next tool.

Hydro jetting in Downey, CA is a drain cleaning method that uses high-pressure water to wash grease, scale, and soft debris off the inside of the pipe. It treats the pipe wall, which is where a repeat clog actually lives.

Signs jetting may be the right next step

  • 1A main drain that has been cabled more than once in the past year or two.
  • 2Slow drains on several fixtures even though no single clog has been found.
  • 3A kitchen line with a long history of grease, or a laundry line with heavy lint.
  • 4A camera view showing a coating of buildup along the pipe wall.
  • 5A cable that cannot pass a section it should be able to clear.

Honest Limits

When we will not jet a line

Jetting is not a cure-all. If the camera shows a cracked pipe, a pipe that has collapsed, a belly where water pools, or joints that have separated, adding high-pressure water can make the damage worse. We stop in those cases and recommend sewer line repairs or, for a line that has failed along a long stretch, sewer line replacement.

The Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) sets minimum slopes for drain lines. A pipe that has settled below that slope holds water, and debris collects in the low spot, so jetting alone cannot cure a sag.

We also do not promise that a jetted line will stay clear forever. A cleaned pipe that still receives grease from the kitchen will build up again. What jetting buys you is a clean starting point and, for most homes, a longer interval before the next call. Smart habits at the sink protect that.
