Signs It May Be Time

Rusty water, weak pressure, and leaks that keep returning

Pipes rarely fail without warning. Water that runs brown or rusty when you first open a tap, pressure that fades across the house, blue-green stains near fittings, and repeated pinhole leaks are signs that the supply pipe is wearing out from the inside.

Another hint is the repair history. If you have fixed three leaks in two years, the next one is already forming. Each repair buys a few months on a system that is failing everywhere, and the opened walls add to the cost.

Repiping in Downey, CA means replacing the water supply pipes inside the house, from the main shutoff to each fixture, with new PEX or copper. It does not cover the underground line from the meter, which is a separate job described on our service line repair page.

Local Picture

What Downey's housing says about repiping

Census-based counts put about 13,400 Downey homes in the 1950s, 3,000 in the 1940s, and roughly 5,800 in the 1960s. Homes built then went up with the materials of their time, and many have been partly repiped since. That mixed history is why we inspect before quoting.

Hard water adds a variable. The city's supply is hard, and scale accumulates in old pipes and can restrict flow. It does not cause every repipe, but it contributes to the pressure complaints we hear.

A repipe is also a natural time to add a pressure regulator. The California Plumbing Code, built on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), expects one above 80 psi, and new pipe should not be stressed by high pressure.

A spot repair

  • One failure on otherwise sound pipe
  • Small opening in the wall
  • Quick and lower cost
  • Does not stop new leaks elsewhere

A repipe

  • Repeated failures or widespread corrosion
  • Opens walls once, on a plan
  • New pipe with a long service life
  • Often resolves low pressure and discoloration
