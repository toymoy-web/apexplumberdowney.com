Shower & Bathtub Installation in Downey, CA

New showers and tubs with the valve, supply, and drain done to code, and a clear line between plumbing and the other trades.

  • Valve to code
  • Leak-tested
  • Permits explained

Shower and bathtub installation in Downey, CA means setting a new shower or tub, with the valve, the supply lines, and the drain done to code. Most projects are a swap of an old tub for a new shower, a new tub in the same spot, or a replacement valve with new trim. We handle the plumbing and tell you where other trades begin.

A good installation is mostly the parts you will never see again: the valve behind the wall, the trap under the floor, and the connections that have to stay tight for decades. Getting them right takes an hour of planning and saves opening the wall a second time.

What the plumbing side of an installation includes

  • 1A pressure-balancing or thermostatic shower valve rated to ASSE 1016.
  • 2New supply lines, with shutoffs reachable for later repairs.
  • 3A drain and trap sized and sloped to code.
  • 4A showerhead connection that meets California's 1.8 gallons per minute limit.
  • 5A leak test before walls or floors are closed up.
  • 6A permit and inspection when the work requires one.

Downey Conditions

Hard water and older bathrooms

Scale builds on showerheads and inside shower valve cartridges, which is why a showerhead can lose its spray and a cartridge can stick within a few years. A softener addresses the cause, and a vinegar soak clears a showerhead.

Census-based counts put roughly 13,400 Downey homes in the 1950s. Bathrooms in houses that age often still hold original plumbing behind the walls, so a remodel is a good moment to replace it while the wall is open.

The California Plumbing Code, built on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), sets the rules for valves, traps, and drains. We follow it, and a permitted job is inspected before the wall is closed.

Questions

Shower and bathtub questions from Downey

The plumbing side includes the shower valve, the supply lines to it, the drain and trap, and the showerhead connection. Tile, waterproofing membranes, and wall surfaces are usually separate trades. We tell you where our scope ends so the project is coordinated.

The California Plumbing Code expects shower valves to be pressure-balancing, thermostatic, or a combination type, which keeps the water from suddenly going hot or cold when someone flushes a toilet elsewhere. Look for valves that meet ASSE 1016.

California limits new showerheads to 1.8 gallons per minute, lower than the federal limit. Many modern heads deliver a satisfying spray at that rate. Hard water scale can make any head perform worse over time, so cleaning matters.

It can. A cast iron tub is heavy, and a full tub of water adds more. We check the framing and the subfloor before setting one. Acrylic and fiberglass tubs are lighter but still need proper support underneath.

Changing a shower or tub, moving a drain, or replacing a valve behind a wall generally needs a permit. A like-for-like fixture swap may not. We tell you what applies before work starts.

It starts as diagnosis. A leak behind a wall may be the valve, a supply fitting, the drain, or the pan. We find the source first, then recommend a repair or a full replacement. Our leak detection page explains how.

Hard water hurts fixtures, so consider a softener repair. If the old shower already leaks behind the wall, start with leak tracing. Related work includes faucet repair, and we install showers in Paramount as well.

Showers in Downey, Los Angeles County, run on water from the City of Downey Water Division, and where it runs hard that clogs showerheads and valve cartridges within a few years. Homes from Wilderness Park to the Columbia Memorial Space Center can slow that with a vinegar soak or a softener.
