Start With Your Reason

Chlorine taste, cloudy glasses, or wanting to know what is in your water

Most people who look into filtration have one of a few reasons. The water tastes or smells of chlorine. A glass looks cloudy when first poured.

A family member wants cleaner drinking water. Or a news story or a utility letter raised a question about a specific contaminant.

Each reason points to a different tool, and a filter that solves one may do nothing for another. Starting with the reason keeps you from buying a system that solves a problem you do not have.

Water filtration installation in Downey, CA means choosing and installing a filter or purification system sized for your household and certified for the thing you want to remove. It can be a single under-sink unit, a reverse osmosis tap, or a whole-house system at the main line.

What Is In Local Water

Why your utility's report comes first

Each utility in this area publishes an annual water quality report listing what it detected. Golden State Water Company's 2026 report for the Norwalk System, for example, says its water purchased from the Metropolitan Water District contains chloramine, and it lists hardness, minerals, and trace constituents with the ranges found.

The City of Downey Water Division operates 20 groundwater wells and keeps three imported MWD connections, so its mix of sources differs from a city that buys all its water. Your own report shows what applies to your address. That is where to start, because a filter chosen for a contaminant your water does not have is money spent for nothing.

We are not medical professionals and we do not make health claims about particular contaminants. A lab test of your own tap is the right tool when you have a specific concern.

Whole-house filter

  • Treats water at the main line
  • Protects every tap and appliance
  • Needs adequate space and bypass valves
  • Best for sediment or chlorine across the house

Point-of-use filter

  • Treats one tap, usually under the sink
  • Lower cost and easy to service
  • Good for drinking and cooking water
  • RO and certified carbon units are common

Questions

Water filtration questions from Downey

A softener removes hardness minerals, calcium and magnesium, to stop scale. A filter removes other things, such as chlorine taste and odor, sediment, or specific contaminants, depending on the type. They solve different problems, and some homes use both. If scale is your issue, see our water softener page. If taste or a specific contaminant is the issue, a filter is the tool.

Start with what you want to remove. For chlorine taste, a carbon filter certified to NSF/ANSI 42 is the usual answer. For specific health-related contaminants such as lead, look for NSF/ANSI 53 certification for that contaminant. For a broad reduction at one tap, reverse osmosis systems certified to NSF/ANSI 58 are common. The certification must list what you care about.

Some systems in this area use imported Metropolitan Water District water, which contains chloramine. Golden State Water's Norwalk report says so. Standard carbon removes chlorine more easily than chloramine, so if chloramine is present you want a filter designed for it. Check your utility's report to see what is added.

Some people add a remineralizing stage for taste. It is a good fit for a household that wants one very clean tap, and overkill for one that only dislikes a chlorine taste.
